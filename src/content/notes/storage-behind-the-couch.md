We recently got new windows and trim, and new window treatments are being
installed soon. That meant the couch couldn't stay pushed up against the wall
any longer — we didn't want to risk it hitting the treatments. So the sectional
came off the walls a few inches, and suddenly there was a gap behind it. At our
house we're constantly trying to maximize space, and I happened to have some
rift-sawn white oak plywood laying around from another project. You can see
where this is going.

The plan: a ledge along the back for drinks and charging, and a bin along the
side for laptops, phones, books, and magazines. Both pieces sit just under the
window trim, hidden behind the couch back, but within easy reach from the seat.

## The drink ledge

The ledge runs 90½" along the window wall — 5" deep, with a raised front lip so
drinks can't slide off the edge. I cut out a spot for a recessed power strip —
outlets and USB ports sitting flush with the shelf — so phones can charge
without a cord draped over the back of the couch. The
whole thing rests on L-brackets screwed into the studs.

I had initially wanted proper cup holders: 3" circles drilled about ¼" deep
with a forstner bit, just enough of a pocket to keep a mug from wandering.
Either I purchased cheap forstner bits or that's really a drill press job,
because I couldn't get anywhere near the clean look I wanted — and I wasn't
going to find out on the finished shelf. The lip stays; the pockets can wait.

## The device bin

The bin runs 44½" along the side wall and holds everything upright in a single
1½" lane. The trick is the floor: one side is deeper than the other. The deep
side swallows laptops and iPads, and the shallow side keeps phones, books, and
magazines riding high enough to grab without fishing around. Holes in the
floors let charging cables come up from below, so devices charge while they're
docked. It hangs on a metal french cleat, so it lifts straight off the wall for
cleaning.

## Making it fit

The whole build was designed around the room's real constraints: a 1" window
trim overhang above, a 25" couch back in front, and not much space behind.
Every exposed plywood edge got iron-on white oak edge banding, and both pieces
got a warm honey stain to match the couch's legs and a coat of satin
water-based polyurethane. The couch now sits just a few inches off the walls,
and the gap finally earns its keep. The whole build, from ripping sheets to
the finished corner, is in the gallery below.
